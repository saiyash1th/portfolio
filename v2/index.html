Who I Am

Supply Chain Leader
Driven by Data. Built for Scale.

I started out nerdy and entirely in my comfort zone — and realized at some point that I needed to step out of it. Since then, I’ve never looked back.

I’ve worn many hats over the course of my career, but every role has led me deeper into becoming a well-rounded supply chain professional. My experience has given me near-complete end-to-end supply chain operations exposure — from network design and operational setup through distribution and program closeout — and the complexity I’ve navigated has sharpened my stakeholder management, strategic communication, and cross-functional leadership capabilities significantly.

What I’m proudest of: I’ve never settled for the bare minimum, and I’ve never accepted “that’s how it’s always been done” as a reason not to improve. My belief is simple — supply chain gets better when it’s blended with the right technology and tools. I’ve consistently sought out ways to automate repetitive work, cut waste, and drive measurable cost and efficiency improvements.

I have been described as someone who can be assigned fewer resources because I manage efficiently and reliably. That’s the standard I hold myself to.

My next step is a senior supply chain strategy, planning, or analytics-focused role where I can set high-level processes, leverage analytical and AI tools at scale, and optimize how organizations think about and run their supply chains — across pharma, FMCG, manufacturing, technology, or any data-intensive environment.

Product Labelling Crisis — Compressed Recovery Timeline
Supply Chain Program Lead • Global Operations
SituationA critical product labelling error was discovered only 8 weeks before scheduled distribution — less than half the standard 16–20 week lead time. Product was destined for temperature-sensitive distribution across multiple markets.
ActionImmediately took ownership and escalated to program leadership. Identified bottlenecks in quality approval and invoicing workflows and fast-tracked them. Ran packaging and distribution workstreams in parallel rather than sequentially to compress the timeline.
ResultCompleted packaging in 4 weeks and distribution in another 4 weeks — full delivery within 7.5 weeks with zero impact on end customers or program timelines. Learnings codified into risk mitigation frameworks for future programs.

Regulatory Permit Delay — Protecting End-Customer Supply
Supply Chain Program Lead • Global Distribution
SituationAn import permit delay in a key market threatened product shortages for end customers. Existing inventory was approaching its shelf life limit, and the distribution system had built-in controls that restricted shipment once inventory age crossed a threshold.
ActionEvaluated two options: adjusting individual inventory records vs. changing the global system parameter. Carefully modeled the downstream risk of each option across all markets. Advocated for the targeted inventory-level fix, providing analytical rationale that it would not compromise product integrity in any other market.
ResultOption approved. Product availability maintained until the permit was secured. Zero disruption to customer supply. Risk analysis framework applied to subsequent regulatory-constraint scenarios.
