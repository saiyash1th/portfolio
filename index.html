My manager has put it simply: he can hand me more projects because he knows they will get done efficiently, reliably, and faster than most — without compromising on quality. That’s the standard I hold myself to.

My next step is a planning, strategy, or technology-focused senior role where I can set high-level processes, leverage analytical and AI tools at scale, and optimize how organizations think about and run their supply chains.

Supply chain rarely goes to plan. The real test is how you respond when it does not. Here is a selection of complex, high-stakes situations I navigated — and the outcomes I drove.

Crisis Recovery
Critical Labelling Error — 8-Week Recovery Window
Clinical Supply Study Lead • AstraZeneca
A labelling error discovered just 8 weeks before distribution — less than half the standard 16–20 week lead time — with patient dosing schedules directly at stake.
SituationA serious labelling error packs with a 30-month shelf life labelled with a 36-month expiry. Discovered only 8 weeks before distribution was needed, against a standard 16–20 week timeline.
ActionImmediately took ownership, escalated simultaneously to clinical and program leads. Identified QA approval and invoicing as the key bottlenecks and fast-tracked both in parallel. Coordinated packing and distribution workstreams concurrently to compress the total timeline.
ResultPacking completed in 4 weeks, distribution in a further 4 weeks — full delivery within 7.5 weeks with zero impact on patient dosing schedules. Learnings codified into risk mitigation frameworks for future studies.

Regulatory & Supply Risk
Import License Delay — Drug Shortage Risk at Clinical Sites
Clinical Supply Study Lead • the study
An Import License delay left existing stock approaching expiry, with IRT DND rules threatening to cut off patient access before replacement stock could arrive.
Situationan Import License delay put drug availability at risk. Existing stock neared expiry and IRT DND rules (blocking dispensing within 30 days of expiry) compounded the risk. Two options: extend individual kit expiry dates, or reduce the global DND period.
ActionModelled both options. Reducing DND globally would introduce risk across all countries. Advocated strongly for the targeted kit extension, providing clear clinical and regulatory rationale that it posed no patient safety risk.
ResultTeam agreed. Drug availability maintained at all sites until the IL was secured. No patient continuity disruption. Risk analysis framework applied to future high-stakes decisions.
