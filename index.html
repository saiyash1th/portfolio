Who I Am

Clinical Supply Chain Leader
Driven by Data. Powered by Innovation.

I started out nerdy and entirely in my comfort zone — and realized at some point that I needed to step out of it. Since then, I’ve never looked back.

I’ve worn many hats over the course of my career, but every role has led me deeper into becoming a well-rounded supply chain professional. My experience at AstraZeneca has given me near-complete E2E clinical supply study management exposure — from protocol design through study closeout — and the operational complexity I’ve navigated has sharpened my stakeholder management, communication, and cross-functional leadership capabilities significantly.

What I’m proudest of: I’ve never settled for the bare minimum, and I’ve never accepted “that’s how it’s always been done” as a reason not to improve. My belief is simple — supply chain gets better when it’s blended with the right technology and tools. I’ve consistently sought out ways to automate repetitive work, cut waste, and drive measurable cost and efficiency improvements.

My manager has described me as someone he can assign fewer FTEs to because he knows I’ll manage efficiently. That’s the standard I hold myself to.

My next step is a planning, strategy, or technology-focused senior role where I can set high-level processes, leverage analytical and AI tools at scale, and optimize how organizations think about and run their supply chains.

Kalos Labelling Crisis — Hot Country Emergency
Clinical Supply Study Lead • AstraZeneca
SituationA serious labelling error in the Kalos study: packs with a 30-month shelf life were labelled with a 36-month expiry. Discovered only 8 weeks before distribution was needed, against a standard 16–20 week timeline.
ActionImmediately took ownership — escalated to clinical team and program leads simultaneously. Identified QA and invoice approval bottlenecks and fast-tracked them. Coordinated packing and distribution teams in parallel to recover maximum time.
ResultCompleted packing in 4 weeks and distribution in a further 4 weeks — all materials delivered within 7.5 weeks. Zero impact on patient dosing schedules. Learnings applied to future study risk frameworks.

We Put Patients First
Vietnam Import License Delay — IRT DND Risk
Clinical Supply Study Lead • Kalos Study
SituationVietnam Import License delay in the Kalos study threatened drug shortages at clinical sites. Existing stock was nearing expiry and the IRT system’s Do Not Dispense (DND) period — blocking dispensing within 30 days of expiry — compounded the risk.
ActionExplored two options with the IRT team: manually extending kit expiry dates vs. reducing the DND period. Carefully weighed that reducing DND would affect all countries. Advocated strongly for the kit extension option, providing clinical and regulatory rationale to reassure the team it posed no patient safety risk.
ResultTeam agreed with the approach. Drug availability was maintained across sites until the IL was secured. Patient continuity was never disrupted. Principles applied to future high-stakes supply decisions.
